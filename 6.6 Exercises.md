**Exercise 6.7.**
(a) If e is the identity element, then what is <e>? The same group
(b) If a is in the subgroup <b, c>, then what is <a, b, c>? <a, b, c> = <b, c>

know about the order of <a, b, c>? Then the order of <a, b, c> is a multiplier of 28.

**Exercise 6.11.** A particular special case of Lagrange’s Theorem bears highlighting on
its own. As we learned in Chapter 5, any element g in a group can be used to create a
cyclic subgroup called the orbit of g. We write |g| as shorthand for the size of that orbit
(i.e., |g| = |<g>|), and call it the order of g.
Explain why the order |g| of any element must divide the order |G| of the group.
⁠By Lagrange's theorem.

**Exercise 6.12.** Answer each of the following questions and explain why your answers are correct.
(a) How many subgroups does G have when |G| is prime? Two, trivial and G.
(b) What is the orbit of an element in such a group G? The whole group or e if element is e.
(c) How many groups of order p are there, when p is prime? One.
